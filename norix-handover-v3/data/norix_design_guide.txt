NATURALS PALETTE

The colors from our Naturals Palette have been carefully selected to
support a therapeutic environment that helps reduce tension, assists in
behavior modification and enhances the safety of both residents and staff.


Research indicates that interiors with an interesting use of color that are not overly
neutral can:

• Balance the mental equilibrium of residents
• Reduce behavioral discord
• Promote dignity and encourage socialization
• Create relaxing environments to enable rest and encourage faster healing
• In general, improve resident and staff morale

When applied with understanding and purpose, color can be used to promote self-
confidence, uplift the spirit, soothe the psyche and be a positive distraction among
residents. The colors in the Natural Palette have shown to address three
primary human elements:

• Familiarity – A sense of connection with the outside world.
• Stress Reduction – Calming and lifting the human spirit.
• Morale Improvement – Humanizing and supporting a feeling of normality.

Each color option in the Naturals Palette is designed to work together to deliver a
seamless transition from room to room and promote a sense of health, well-being and
sustainability throughout the entire facility.

PATIENT ROOM

By incorporating natural colors and textures into patient rooms, facilities can establish an environment that
fosters a healing atmosphere, enhances patient comfort, and supports the therapeutic process. Earthy
greens, like Sage, help diffuse patient anxiety and soften their transition to a new environment. A warm,
homelike atmosphere can be established by implementing furniture constructed using our unique TruGrain™
polymer formulation, a highly durable and easy to clean polymer designed to mimic the warmth of
natural wood.

WELCOMING & WAITING

A good first impression can set the tone for a patient’s entire stay. The strategic use of design elements in
welcoming and waiting areas, like the use of natural light, soothing colors and comforting textures, help to
relax the mind and body. The incorporation of plants and natural wood tones creates a connection to nature
that helps blur the lines between interior and exterior, reducing patient apprehension and agitation.

VISITATION

A well-designed visitation area can improve the overall perception of the facility, both among visitors and
within the broader community. The considerate use of color and the integration of organic artwork helps
increase morale and reduces the potential for conflict. Access to natural light has been shown to improve
mood and enhance well-being. Effective use of natural lighting also creates an opportunity to rely less on
artificial lighting, which can save on energy costs and reduce the environmental impact of the facility.

DAYROOM

As the area in which residents spend the majority of their waking hours, it’s vital for dayrooms to closely
reflect a normalized environment that mirrors life outside the facility. An open-plan concept that provides
multiple settings for use throughout the day can help support residents on their journey towards
rehabilitation while still maintaining high levels of safety, security and cleanliness. Earthy greens, neutral
accents and simple pattern flooring help to promote a calm, relaxed environment.

DINING

Dining areas should employ a combination of design elements to create a warm and welcoming ambiance that
allows residents to feel more at ease in a space that may be unfamiliar to them. An open floor plan, coupled with
the application of contrasting colors, can help combat a perception of crowdedness in these areas by adding
visual interest and depth. The installation of furniture with wood accents helps provide a touch of natural beauty
and a sense of familiarity that fosters a homey atmosphere. Patterned fabrics can be used to add energy and
liveliness to the space, supporting a pleasant environment for residents to gather and socialize.

GROUP ROOM

Transitional housing facilities are faced with the daunting task of establishing a comforting and supportive
environment that caters to the needs of several individuals at once. Areas like group rooms can achieve a
warm, homelike atmosphere by incorporating a variety of design concepts, such as utilizing furniture with
wood accents or displaying relaxing wall murals. To mitigate feelings of crowding, flooring should feature
neutral color tones and be supported by an abundance of natural light.
