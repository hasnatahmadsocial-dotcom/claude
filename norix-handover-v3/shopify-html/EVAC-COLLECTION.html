Evacuation Chairs for Stairs, Evacuation Sleds, and Rescue Stretchers

An evacuation chair moves a person who cannot walk down the stairs safely when the elevators are out of service, during a fire, power outage, earthquake, or medical emergency. Anchortex carries Evac+Chair stair evacuation chairs, powered stair climbing chairs, evacuation sleds, and accessories, along with Rapid Extraction (REX) rescue stretchers, for hospitals, nursing homes, schools, offices, high-rise buildings, and correctional facilities.

Most evac chair models are operated by one person: the passenger sits in a secure seat while the operator guides the chair down the stairs on tracks or belts that control the speed. Some models also carry a passenger up the stairs. An evacuation chair can be used in place of an evacuation wheelchair on stairs, where a standard wheelchair cannot go.

Types of Evacuation Equipment

  • Emergency evacuation chairs (manual): Evac+Chair 300H, 400H, 600H, and 750H stair evacuation chairs for controlled travel down the stairs, with weight capacities from 400 to 500 lbs. The 600H and 750H also have extra handles so two people can carry a passenger up the stairs.
  • Powered stair climbing chairs: The battery powered Evac+Chair Power 800 and Power 900 carry a passenger up or down the stairs with one operator, for emergency evacuation and daily patient transport.
  • Stair transport seat: The IBEX 700H Tran-Seat lets two people move a patient up and down the stairs without carrying or heavy lifting.
  • Evacuation sleds and mats: The Evac+Sled, the Ulti-Mat (standard and bariatric sizes), and the Evac+Sheet rescue sheet, which stays under the mattress, for moving people who cannot sit, or bedridden patients, down the stairs.
  • Rescue stretchers: REX ONE, REX DCS with descent control for stairs and steep ground, and REX DCS/CF for correctional facilities. Each folds in three for storage and lets one responder move a patient.
  • Accessories: Comfy Seat cushions, the Transporter seat for transfers from a wheelchair, metal storage cabinets, anti-theft alarms, and the iEvac smoke and fire escape hood.

How to Choose an Evacuation Chair

  • Weight capacity: Check the rated capacity against the people who may need it. The 750H is rated to 500 lbs, and the Ulti-Mat comes in a bariatric size
  • Operators: Most models need one trained operator; some need two, and some allow two-person carrying when needed
  • Direction of travel: Manual evacuation chairs are made for going down the stairs. For going up, choose a powered stair chair, the IBEX 700H, or a model with carry handles
  • Stairways and storage: Measure the stair landings and plan where each chair will be kept. Wall mounting and storage cabinets keep chairs close to the stairs and ready to use
  • Training and planning: Train staff on the chair, practice regularly, and include it in the building's emergency action plan

Evacuation Chair FAQs

What is an evacuation chair?

A lightweight, folding stair chair that lets one or two operators move a person with limited mobility down the stairs, and with some models up the stairs, during an emergency.

How many people does it take to operate an evac chair?

Most manual Evac+Chair models and the powered Power 800 and Power 900 need one operator. The IBEX 700H needs two. Models with carry handles can be lifted by two people.

Can an evacuation chair go up the stairs?

Yes, with the right model. The powered Power 800 and Power 900 and the IBEX 700H go up and down the stairs. The 600H and 750H can be carried up by two people.

Where should evacuation chairs be kept?

Near the stairways, on a wall mount or in a storage cabinet, so staff can reach them quickly.
