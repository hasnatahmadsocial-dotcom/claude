# How I optimize a page (Evac+Chair / REX keyword pilot)

1. **Keywords (Ricky's sheet):** take the primary keyword, secondary keywords and Ricky's note for the page. The primary goes in the H1 or the first sentence, the SEO title, the meta description, one heading, and the FAQs. Secondary keywords go where they read naturally (intro, headings, bullets, FAQs). Nothing is stuffed in where it doesn't fit, and keywords Ricky reserved for another page are not reused.
2. **Selling points (Andrew):** add Andrew's points and notes (for example, we don't sell an evacuation wheelchair, so that term is used only to explain what an evacuation chair does instead).
3. **Competitor research:** search the primary keywords and review the pages that rank (retailers, the manufacturer, and buyer guides). I note:
   - what they cover: types, capacity, number of operators, up or down the stairs, storage, training, codes
   - whether they have FAQs, and which questions
   - which keywords they use in their product names
4. **Facts:** every spec comes from our own product pages and the manufacturer's website or spec sheet. Where these disagree, I leave the number out and list the conflict in the notes for Andrew.
5. **Write:**
   - Page structure: a clear H1, a short intro, scannable bullets, H2 sections (types, how to choose, FAQs, about the brand), and links to our collection pages only.
   - SEO title of 50–65 characters and meta description of 140–160 characters.
   - Copy reads like a person wrote it, with no claims comparing us to other brands.
6. **Review:** the draft goes into the Google Doc with notes and questions. Andrew comments, and Ricky reviews how the keywords and selling points were used.
7. **Publish and check:** I update Shopify one page at a time, keeping the URL, SKUs and prices. Then I check the live page against the approved draft: text, SEO fields, headings, links and weights.

## Collection page pilot: what the research found
- **Who ranks for "evacuation chair" and "evacuation chairs for stairs":**
  - manufacturers: Stryker, Evac+Chair
  - retailers: Grainger, Rehabmart, Global Industrial, Staples
  - university and city buyer guides (the NYC Mayor's Office for People with Disabilities, UC Berkeley)
- **Retailer pages:** they put the capacity in the product name (for example "Evacuation Chair, 500 lb. Capacity"). Their category copy is thin, and the pages we could read had no FAQs.
- **Buyer guides and the manufacturer FAQ:** they answer the same questions every time:
  - how many operators
  - can it go up the stairs
  - capacity
  - stairway type
  - storage
  - training
  - emergency action plans
- **What this page adds:** a "Types" section, a "How to choose" section, and four FAQs based on those questions, all answered with facts from our product pages and the Evac+Chair website.
- **Next for product pages:** consider adding the capacity to product names or subtitles, as the retailers do (Andrew to decide).
